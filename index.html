      About this journey

      Everything here is true to scale and in real time. One pixel is about 2,300 km, so the Sun is roughly 600 pixels wide, Earth is 5½ pixels, and light crosses the screen at about 130 pixels per second.

      The distance changes through the year because Earth’s orbit is slightly oval. Today’s distance is worked out from Earth’s orbit, so the trip takes between about 8 min 7 s (early January) and 8 min 24 s (early July).

      The planets are lined up along the beam for the show. In reality they’re almost never in a row, but each one sits at its true average distance from the Sun.

      Sizes, distances and speeds come from NASA fact sheets. Parker Solar Probe’s record is its December 2024 pass, 6.1 million km from the Sun’s surface at about 192 km/s.
